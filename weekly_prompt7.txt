Question:
In Chapter 14, you will note that in the code example for the Button class, 
the author has included a triple-quoted strings for the class, and functions. 
In fact, in most of the code provided in the book, triple-quoted strings have 
been provided for modules, methods, functions and classes. Discuss how these 
docstrings help programmers with their workflow. Remember your experience and 
the internet are your friends.

Answer:
Docstrings are important for both you, helping you keep track of what 
different classes and functions do in your own code, but also for collaboration, 
to help outsiders who may work with your code understand the different classes 
and functions, how they work, and what they do. In terms of docstring's usefulness 
for your future self, they are very important for large projects. It's very easy to lose 
track of classes and functions with a multi-file project. No matter how many notes 
you take, and how you track everything, having docstrings hardwired into your code 
can help IDEs work harder for you and help speed up the coding process. Once you 
have a project that you want to publish and allow others to use, edit, or debug, 
docstrings are vital to their understanding of your code and will help them 
navigate multiple files without needing to study your code and waste precious 
time tracing functions and methods to understand what action they're each performing.
